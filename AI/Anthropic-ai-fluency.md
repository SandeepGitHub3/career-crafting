## 1. Course Introduction

- This course focuses on **human–AI collaboration**, not just understanding AI as a technology.
- **AI Fluency** means engaging with AI systems effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely.
- The AI Fluency Framework centers on the **"4D" competencies**: Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence.
- The goal is to develop lasting skills that remain relevant as AI technology evolves.
- Effective AI collaboration requires both practical skills *and* a fundamental shift in how we think about working with AI.

---

## 2. Three Ways of Engaging with AI

| Mode | Description |
|---|---|
| **Automation** | The AI completes specific tasks based on your instructions. |
| **Augmentation** | You and AI collaborate as creative thinking and task execution partners. |
| **Agency** | You configure AI to work independently on your behalf — establishing its knowledge and behavior patterns rather than just giving it specific tasks. |

These three modes apply across all four competencies of the framework.

The framework consists of four core competencies:

- **Delegation** — Deciding what work to do with AI vs. yourself
- **Description** — Communicating effectively with AI systems
- **Discernment** — Evaluating AI outputs critically
- **Diligence** — Ensuring responsible AI collaboration

These competencies apply across all three modes (Automation, Augmentation, Agency) and prepare you for evolving AI capabilities.

## 4. Generative AI Fundamentals — Capabilities & Limitations

### What it is

- Generative AI **creates new content** (text, images, code) rather than just analyzing existing data.

### Three key developments that made modern systems possible

1. **Algorithmic / architectural breakthroughs** (especially the transformer architecture)
2. **Vast amounts of digital training data**
3. **Dramatic increases in computational power**

### How it learns — two stages

- **Pre-training** — analyzing patterns across billions of examples
- **Fine-tuning** — learning to follow instructions and provide helpful responses

### Current capabilities

- Versatility across tasks
- Conversational awareness
- Ability to connect with external tools

### Current limitations

- Knowledge cutoff dates
- Potential for hallucinations
- Context window constraints
- Challenges with complex reasoning

### Bottom line

The most effective applications combine human and AI strengths — humans provide critical thinking, judgment, creativity, and ethical oversight.

## 5. Delegation (1st D)

> Delegation is about making thoughtful decisions about what work to do yourself, what to do together with AI, or what to let AI handle independently — and how to distribute those tasks.

### Three sub-competencies

- **Problem Awareness** — clearly understanding your goals and the nature of the work *before* involving AI.
- **Platform Awareness** — understanding the capabilities and limitations of different AI systems.
- **Task Delegation** — thoughtfully distributing work between humans and AI to leverage the strengths of each.

### Key principles

- Effective delegation requires both **domain expertise** and **understanding of AI capabilities**.
- The goal isn't to automate everything — it's to create the most effective human–AI partnership for any given task or goal.

## 6. Description (2nd D)

> Description is about communicating with AI in ways that create a productive collaborative environment.

### Three sub-competencies

- **Product Description** — clearly defining what you want in terms of outputs, format, audience, and style.
- **Process Description** — guiding *how* the AI approaches your request (often as important as specifying the end goal).
- **Performance Description** — defining behavioral aspects: should the AI be concise or detailed? Challenging or supportive?

### Key principles

- AI systems are **interactive partners**, not databases or vending machines.
- Clear communication up front saves time and leads to better results.

## 7. Prompt Engineering — Practical Techniques

Effective prompting combines clear communication principles with AI-specific techniques.

### Six foundational techniques

1. **Give context** — Be specific about what you want, why you want it, and relevant background.
2. **Show examples** — Demonstrate the output style or format you're looking for.
3. **Specify constraints** — Clearly define format, length, and other output requirements.
4. **Break complex tasks into steps** — Guide the AI through multi-step reasoning.
5. **Ask the AI to think first** — Give space for the AI to work through its process.
6. **Define the AI's role or tone** — Specify how you want the AI to communicate.

### The "secret weapon"

Ask the AI itself to help improve your prompt.

### Mindset

- Successful prompting is **iterative** (and often collaborative with the AI).
- Expect to refine your approach based on results.
- Common successful patterns: clear task overviews, format specifications, explicit constraints, relevant background information.

### Connection to the framework

These six techniques are the **practical toolkit** for the Description competency. Description tells you *what* to communicate (Product / Process / Performance); these techniques tell you *how* to actually do it in a prompt.

### Reflection prompts (from the course)

- Which of the six techniques would most enhance your current AI interactions?
- Think of a recent AI interaction that didn't meet your needs — which techniques might have improved the outcome?
- How does understanding these techniques connect to the Description competency?

## 8. Discernment (3rd D)

> Discernment is your ability to thoughtfully evaluate what AI produces, how it produces it, and how it behaves.

### Three sub-competencies

- **Product Discernment** — evaluating the **quality of outputs** (accuracy, appropriateness, coherence, relevance).
- **Process Discernment** — assessing **how the AI arrived at its output** (looking for logical errors, attention gaps, inappropriate reasoning).
- **Performance Discernment** — evaluating **how the AI behaves within the collaboration itself** (is its communication style effective for your needs?).

### Key principles

- Discernment works **hand-in-hand with Description** in a continuous feedback loop.
- Even the most advanced AI systems benefit from human judgment and oversight.

### Pattern to notice

Discernment and Description **mirror each other** — both have Product / Process / Performance as their three lenses. You *describe* what you want along three dimensions, then *discern* whether the AI delivered along those same three dimensions.

---

## 9. Diligence (4th D)

> Diligence is about taking responsibility for our AI collaborations.

### Three sub-competencies

- **Creation Diligence** — being thoughtful about *which AI systems we use* and *how we engage* with them.
- **Transparency Diligence** — being honest about AI's role in our work with everyone who needs to know.
- **Deployment Diligence** — taking responsibility for verifying and vouching for the outputs we use or share.

### Key principles

- Different contexts (personal, academic, professional) may have different expectations for **disclosure and verification**.
- Thoughtful Diligence helps ensure our AI collaborations are not only effective and efficient, but also **ethical and safe**.

---

## 10. Key Takeaways

### The framework's elegant structure

Three of the four Ds break down into three sub-types each, with **Description and Discernment sharing the same three lenses** (Product / Process / Performance). This isn't coincidence — you describe along these dimensions, then evaluate along the same dimensions. That's the feedback loop.

| **Delegation** | Problem Awareness · Platform Awareness · Task Delegation |
