## Story 1: Deliveroo – Restaurant Churn Project

**Situation**  
- Only senior backend engineer in a Salesforce-heavy team.  
- Leading 3 junior developers on the **restaurant churn project**.  
- Returned from a month-long marriage leave → rollout struggling.  
- Restaurant Account Managers questioning why certain accounts weren’t being churned.  
- Juniors had implemented the solution but were hesitant to engage directly with stakeholders.  
- All queries funneled through me → created bottleneck, pulling me away from a **cross-team data retention project (GDPR compliance)**.  

**Task**  
- Stabilize churn rollout and ensure stakeholder concerns addressed.  
- Develop juniors into **confident, independent contributors** able to interact directly with stakeholders.  
- Remove myself as single point of contact to create a **scalable, resilient team structure**.  
- Free my bandwidth for **higher-impact architectural work** (data retention project across multiple teams).  

**Action**  
- Encouraged juniors to handle queries directly → reminded them they built the system.  
- Facilitated initial conversations, then stepped back, only guiding when needed.  
- Framed stakeholder engagement as **career growth opportunity** for them.  
- Applied same approach to data retention project: coached them on how to approach other engineering teams, what technical details to focus on.  
- Took a **step-back leadership approach** → support in background, juniors in front.  

**Result**  
- Within 3 weeks, juniors independently handling all churn queries.  
- Account Managers recognized them as go-to experts → improved trust, faster answers.  
- Juniors gained visibility across org, initiated cross-team discussions themselves.  
- Bottleneck on me eliminated → I redirected full focus to **GDPR data retention initiative**.  
- Created more **resilient, scalable team structure**.  
- Learned Staff-level leadership often means **stepping back strategically to empower others**.

### Paragraph Version

At Deliveroo, I was the only senior backend engineer leading a team of three junior developers on the restaurant churn project. When I returned from a month-long marriage leave, I discovered the initial rollout was struggling—Restaurant Account Managers were questioning why certain accounts weren’t being churned as expected. While the juniors had implemented the solution correctly, they were hesitant to engage directly with stakeholders, so every query was funneled through me. This created a significant bottleneck that not only consumed hours of my time each day but also pulled me away from higher-priority work, including a cross-team data retention project involving coordination across Riders, Consumers, and Restaurants teams.  

I realized that if I continued being the single point of contact, the team would remain fragile and I’d never free up bandwidth for the architectural work I was supposed to drive. Instead, I needed to fundamentally shift my leadership approach—developing these juniors into confident, independent contributors who could handle stakeholder interactions themselves. The goal wasn’t just to unblock me, but to create a more resilient, scalable structure for the entire team.  

I started by encouraging the juniors to handle churn-related queries directly. I pointed out that since they had implemented the entire solution while I was away, they were just as knowledgeable as me. For the first queries, I facilitated the conversation and then deliberately stepped back, providing guidance only when needed. Over time, I pushed them to own these interactions fully, framing it as a valuable growth opportunity that would give them visibility within the organization. In parallel, I applied the same approach to our cross-team data retention project, coaching them on how to initiate conversations with other engineering teams and what technical details to emphasize, while ensuring I was there to support when necessary.  

The transformation was rapid. Within three weeks, the juniors were confidently handling all churn-related queries without any involvement from me. Account Managers began recognizing them as the go-to experts, which not only built stakeholder trust but also gave the juniors direct recognition for their work. They also began proactively engaging with other engineering teams for the data retention project, demonstrating ownership and technical maturity.  

As a result, the team no longer relied on me as a bottleneck. Stakeholders got faster responses, the juniors grew in confidence and visibility, and I was able to fully redirect my focus to the cross-team GDPR compliance initiative. This shift didn’t just solve an immediate bottleneck—it created a more resilient and scalable structure where knowledge and ownership were distributed rather than centralized. The experience reinforced for me that effective leadership at the Staff level often means **stepping back rather than stepping in**, empowering others to own their expertise, and designing processes that scale beyond a single person.
