# Conflict Resolution Stories

## Story 1: J.P. Morgan – Repo Owner Conflict

**Situation**  
- Part of the **Avatar team** at J.P. Morgan, building the next-gen trading platform.  
- Team used a **repo ownership model** where each repo had a designated gatekeeper.  
- One repo owner repeatedly delayed PR reviews, introduced last-minute changes, and slowed down feature delivery.  
- These delays created bottlenecks that not only impacted me, but also affected **dependent teams waiting for changes**.  

**Task**  
- Deliver accruals-related endpoints to support another team’s functionality within the timeline.  
- Find a way to **navigate delays and friction constructively**, without escalating.  
- Ensure the repo became easier for **all contributors to work with**, not just myself.  

**Action**  
- Shifted strategy from pressing for faster reviews to **addressing systemic repo issues**.  
- Fixed **flaky tests** and cleaned up long-standing bugs that had been pain points for multiple engineers.  
- Improved maintainability and reliability of the repo, which benefited **everyone contributing to it**.  
- Raised the quality of my PRs to anticipate concerns, reducing review friction.  
- Introduced improvements and approaches that even helped the repo owner **adopt new practices**.  
- Maintained professionalism and persistence, showing I was invested in making the repo better for the team, not just myself.  

**Result**  
- Repo owner shifted from resistant to **publicly praising my contributions** in a team meeting.  
- Review cycles became smoother, reducing bottlenecks for **future work across the team**.  
- The repo became more stable and reliable, which improved velocity for dependent teams as well.  
- Learned that at Staff level, conflict resolution means **turning localized friction into systemic improvements that uplift the entire team**.

## Story 2: Deliveroo – Polish Colleague Conflict

**Situation**  
- Joined Deliveroo as the **senior-most backend engineer** in a Salesforce-heavy team.  
- Responsible for mentoring **two junior backend engineers**.  
- Depended on a sister backend team that included a Polish engineer — technically very strong, junior in title but highly respected.  
- He acted as a **strict quality gatekeeper**, giving highly detailed and critical PR reviews that often delayed work.  
- Both I and my junior engineers faced challenges dealing with his reviews, which risked delivery timelines and team morale.  

**Task**  
- Deliver a **critical Salesforce-to-backend sync feature** within a two-sprint deadline.  
- Ensure smooth collaboration with the Polish colleague’s team, who owned the backend repos.  
- Enable my juniors to succeed despite the heavy review process.  
- Meet stakeholder expectations without lowering quality.  

**Action**  
- Treated his rigorous standards as a **benchmark for quality**, not an obstacle.  
- Improved my own coding practices: broke down large PRs, added clearer documentation, raised test coverage.  
- Mentored my **junior engineers** on these practices, so they could adapt to the high review bar and structure their work better.  
- Took ownership of the toughest review conversations to **shield juniors from unnecessary friction**.  
- Built rapport with the Polish colleague outside of work (casual conversations), making collaboration more personal and constructive.  

**Result**  
- Successfully delivered the sync feature on time despite extended review cycles.  
- Junior engineers adapted quickly, became more productive, and gained confidence in meeting high standards.  
- Collaboration between our teams became smoother and less adversarial.  
- The **overall quality bar for the team improved**, and juniors directly benefited from my mentorship.  
- Learned that at Staff level, conflict resolution isn’t just about unblocking yourself — it’s about **transforming friction into team-wide growth and protecting less experienced engineers**.
